# A House Of Prayer

## Why I am writing this book

There are words you hear every week on Sunday. Words that you might contemplate, mouth, or in the rush of life, forget. Those words are *"That they may always remember Him."* If you grew up a member of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints or are currently a member, then you will be very familiar with this phrase. I grew up hearing it, repeating it, saying it and familiarizing myself with it.

Other such phrases may come to mind, for example, the prayer for baptism: 

"*[/NAME/] I baptize you in the Name of the Father, and of the Son, and Of the Holy Spirit. Amen."*

These words never change, and are said again and again. If you are a member of the church who goes to the temple, you may notice that the structure retains its form, even if the words may change slightly. The clothing worn, the words said, and the symbol gestures done retain their meaning and purpose, regardless of what else changes.


I do not consider myself a member of your church anymore, or at least in a way that is as meaningful as it once was. While I consider myself culturally Mormon, I am theologically and spiritually Anglo-Catholic/Anglican Catholic or in a euphamistic phrase I'm "between the Thames and the Tiber with a pinky finger in the Great Salt Lake."

When I go to church every Sunday, we too have words that are said the same every week, or have a set of phrases and structured practices that remain the same from week to week, even as the seasons change.

One such snippet remains the same, even as the exact words may shift, the meaning is retained: 

*Sanctify them by your Holy Spirit to be for your people the Body and Blood of your Son, the holy food and drink of new and unending life in him. Sanctify us also that we may faithfully receive this holy Sacrament, and serve you in unity, constancy, and peace; and at the last day bring us with all your saints into the joy of your eternal kingdom."* (Book of Common Prayer, Eucharistic Prayer A)

These practices and words are called **liturgy** and play an important role in religious life. Some religious denominations are more liturgical and structured, others are less so. The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints sits on the "less so" end of things, its structured prayers being limited to just a few snippets and almost all "High Church" liturgy is strictly found in the LDS temple.

While this is not a problem, however I have found in my talking to Members that they often ask the same questions:

- *"How do I make sacrament more meaningful?"*
- *"How do I make my daily prayers and spiritual practice more significant?"*
- *"How do I make the holidays more meaningful?"*
- *"Why do I feel so disconnected from God is my private and family prayers?"*

Having been in and out of the LDS Church, I believe the answer to these rather painful question lies in the power of liturgical praying

## My love letter to the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints

This book is a love letter to the member of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. It is a love letter to the religion I grew up in, to the ward family that raised me. It is a love letter to my 2 years of missionary service, to the Lubbock Temple, where I spent so much of my teenage years and where I eventually was endowed, to the Houston Temple, where I was married to my wife, to Provo (where I live now,) and to my ancestors, who crossed and died on cold windy plains of Wyoming just to get here.

I want you to find meaning and maybe a new routine through this book. and I hope you see it as a supplament to your own religious and spiritual life rather than a supplanting of it.

### A Note: The Trinitarian Elephant in the Room

When I say the words "In the Name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit," **You and I are going to have some very important and key differences in what that means.** Please not that when I use it here, I'm using it in YOUR context, not mine. That means a Social Trinity, a Godhead unified in purpose, but not in essense or body. Hopefully this clears up some confusion moving forward. Please also understand that this simple, one-line distinction is what may separate you from other Christians. 

Please note that I personally percieve you as Christian, that you are followers of Jesus Christ, worthy of His Holy and Mighty Love as children of God and that in no way is my use of the trinitarian formula an attempt to promote my beliefs onto you.

## Some Unique Changes

As the Mormon Movement features unique moments in history that diverge from the rest of Christianity, there are a couple of things that will stick out to non-members of the LDS church.

1. Feasts celebrating the modern prophets and apostles, LDS martyrs, and moments in LDS history.
2. All Hallows Eve and the Hallows Triidum has been expanded into a 10-day period, starting with the Feast of St. James and ending on All Saints Day in order to commemorate a number of martyrs who have perished in the name of the Lord.
3. A unique holiday season in the summer commemorating LDS-specific events. While they do not all fall on their historic day, I did try to fit them together in such a way that allowed for the creation of a new holiday celebrating these unique aspects of LDS culture and belief.
4. General Conference weekend is marked as a two-day floating holiday, the dates of which are set by the Church. They are to be treated as sacred feast days akin to Easter or Christmas.
5. Major feasts celebrating the Restoration.
6. A high-church format for sacrament meetings and other public ordinances, derived from traditional Eucharist services. Temple liturgy has not and will never be included in this document to preserve its sacredness within LDS theology. Likewise, these formats are for edification only, and should only be used as directed by General Authorities and Leaders of the Church. If they are not approved for use, then they exist for YOUR edification only.
8. The Lectionary will include the Book of Mormon, Doctrine and Covenants, and Pearl of Great Price.
