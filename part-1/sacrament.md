## A Note

The Handbook for the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints simply says this for how to conduct a sacrament meeting: 

"Leaders plan and conduct meetings “as they are led by the Holy Ghost, according to the commandments and revelations of God” (Doctrine and Covenants 20:45; see also Moroni 6:9; Doctrine and Covenants 46:2). They seek ways to invite the influence of the Spirit in their meetings."

Beyond this, there is not much clarification given on how an ward in the church can manage, handle, or run a sacrament meeting. However, due to the difference in culture and practice, the sacrament meeting services provided below more than likely should not be used in an actual meeting and instead exists as the foundation for all other services and practices presented in this book.

For Christians worldwide, the most important service is the service of the Eucharist, or what members of the church would call "The Sacrament." Catholics and some Aglicans call it Mass, while others simply call it Communion. Regardless, the format remains the same as it has in ancient times:

Part 1: The Liturgy of the Word

1. The procession
2. Blessing to God/Praise to the Lord
3. A PRayer
4. The Gloria (Glory to God) / Prayer for Mercy and Forgiveness
5. A Prayer (Collect)
6. Gospel Readings and Sermon
7. A Procession of Faith (usually the Nicene)
8. Collective Prayer (Called the Prayers of the People or the Cycle of Prayer)
9. Confession of Sin
10. The Peace (not a kiss anymore)

Part 2: The Liturgy of the Eucharist
1. The Great Thanksgiving
   1. The Blessing of the Bread
   2. The Blessing of the Wine/Water
   3. The Breaking of the Bread
2. The giving of the Eucharist/Sacrament to the congregation
3. A Thanks to God
4. A blessing from the priest
5. The Recession

This service form is incredibly ancient and has its roots in the beginning of the Christian faith.

However, the modified form below fits for the sensbilities of members of the church, who might not be familiar with the useage of altars and sacred vestments outside of the temple. It might appear, to some, to be too "Catholic" for their tastes.

Temple prep classes usually insufficient, and the rest of LDS liturgy simply doesn't match the very "high church" style found in the temple.

Another reason why I want to start here is that everything you see in "old" christianity is built off of, and is centered around, the sacrament. Everything you do is oriented towards this service.
